# Principles — the spine of the standard

Five principles sit above the seven pillars (the seven main pages).
Every pillar, every rule, every page must obey them. If any page
fights a principle, the principle wins — and we fix the page.

## 1. Legibility (easy to read)

If you can't read it, it doesn't count. A rule, a fee, or a promise
only counts if the person it touches can read it and understand it.

*Example: a fee hidden on page 40 of a contract is not a fee you
agreed to — it goes on page one, or it goes away.*

## 2. Verifiability (easy to check)

Claims carry proof. When we say something is true, we attach the
proof — a record, a receipt, a link — so anyone can check it without
asking us first.

*Example: "we gave 10% to the shared fund" comes with the payment
record, not just the sentence.*

## 3. Simplicity

We keep the smallest rule that works. If a shorter rule protects
people just as well, the longer rule is wrong.

*Example: "never print secrets like passwords — only name the files
that hold them" replaces a thirty-page rulebook about secrets.*

## 4. Honesty under failure (we admit it when things break)

We break loudly and fix openly. When something fails, we say so where
everyone can see. Then we publish what broke, what we did, and what
we changed — so it cannot fail the same way twice.

*Example: when payments stop working, the notice goes on the front
page that same day — not in a hidden log.*

## 5. People first

Rules serve people — never the other way around. If a rule hurts the
person it is meant to protect, the rule is wrong, and we change it.

*Example: if a security rule locks a grandmother out of her own
savings, we fix the rule, not the grandmother.*

---

That is the whole spine. Everything else in this standard is these
five principles, put to work.

**The test:**

"Hand this page to a stranger. After one read, can they tell you the
five principles in their own words?"
